4Three questions, three different axes

What students should be able to do, who the work is for, and what actually goes wrong now. These refine what your content has already said rather than deciding it. Tick one in each.

By the end, what should students be able to do differently?
CR — Make something they did not think they could make
RE — Tell sound work from unsound work
CP — Hold their own against a rival or a deadline
SI — Understand the situation from the inside
Who is the thing they walk out with actually for?
Every answer is a person, and the person decides the tag.
CR — A client, or a future employer — someone who wants the work itself
RE — Someone who has to act on it and needs to know it is right
CP — A judge, a panel, or the other team — someone deciding who did better
SI — A learner — someone who understands it only by going through it
What goes wrong most often in student work on this topic?
Answer from grading, not from theory.
RE — It is wrong, and they did not check
CR — It is correct but generic — it could be anyone’s
CP — It reads fine on paper, but they would struggle to do it for real
SI — It is accurate, but they cannot picture where this actually happens

A Lab is any SNHU AI Labs session; the tag says what kind. Your topic kind, your raw material, and how the mistake gets caught do most of the deciding — the three questions on page 2 refine it.

6The four tags

TagWhat students do — and the activities that fitThey walk out with
CR · CreateMaking Use AI to make something tangible, then push past its generic first attempt with their own knowledge.Pick one: the real professional deliverable · an explainer for outsiders · a weak version and a strong one, compared A finished, shareable piece of work — plus the prompt that changed it.
RE · ResearchVerifying AI answers fluently and confidently; students verify it against something real. The error is planted, not hoped for.Pick one, by what they verify against: Execute and Verify — run it and watch it fail · The Data — cleaning is the verification · The Source — trace to a primary origin · Your Expertise — catch AI wrong in your field · AI as Investigator — hunt a planted flaw A conclusion plus its evidence trail — never a summary.
CP · CompeteRacing or rivalry Go head to head with a rival, or race a real clock, with AI driving the pace and the score.Pick one: head-to-head negotiation · a pitch-off · race to working · the adversarial pair · an incoming queue · a shrinking window A decision log — every call made under pressure, and why.
SI · SimulateExploring Build an experience someone else can step into. Depth of understanding shows immediately.Pick one: an hour-by-hour journey · one scene in full detail · a branching path with consequences A navigable scenario another person can learn from.

7The quick read on your answers

If your topic kind was……the tag is usually
A technique, calculation, or standardResearch — and if students catch the mistake by running it, Execute and Verify
Built or designedCreate — unless the point is that it runs and is silently wrong, which is Research
Navigated by peopleSimulate — or Compete if there is a rival or a real clock
Understood or argued aboutResearch — or Simulate for a historical moment students step into
Two rules that override any of the above

No rival and no clock, no Compete. A countdown bolted onto a verification activity is decoration — if the audit survives without the timer, it is Research. And if the artifact is for someone else to move through, it is Simulate, not Create: a Create artifact is for the maker, a Simulate artifact is for a learner.

If it is not ready yet

Two things stop a Lab from building, and both are two minutes of conversation: no planted error, or a topic that is still a whole unit rather than the single thing students most often get wrong. Fix either and it builds.

Every Lab runs on any AI tool a student already has — approved tools only, no real client or personal data — and states in writing what AI produced versus what the students decided.
