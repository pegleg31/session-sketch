Eight skills by nine outputs, 72 cells. Each cell shows what the two content axes alone say about the tag, which failure kind is available, and which of the eleven AI jobs are legal there. Generated from Session-Sketch.html by build-axis-grid.js — the pull numbers are the engine's own.

How to read it

Three things per cell

The tag chip is what these two answers alone say, with the axis-only margin over second place — material, catch-method and competing are not in these numbers, so a thin margin here is normal and not a problem. The failure kind comes from the skill row: whether there is a right answer to plant an error against. The job chips are filled where the job is a natural fit, outlined where it works but needs reframing, and struck through with a dashed edge where it is ruled out — hover for the reason. Every one of the eleven jobs is drawn in every cell, so an absence is always deliberate; use Hide ruled-out jobs for a compact read. Cells with a gold left edge carry one of the twelve regression cases.

What the grid shows

Read these first

1. The Oracle covers exactly half the grid

It is legal in 36 of 72 cells and illegal in 36. Three rows have no right answer to plant an error against — make, situation, judgment — and the fourth, interpret, is contested by nature, so the honest job there is The Witness cross-examining provenance rather than an oracle being wrong about a conclusion. The tool currently produces an Oracle activity for all 72 cells.

2. 27 cells can only fail flat, not wrong

Every cell in the make, situation and judgment rows. On those topics the honest failure is that AI produced the average of everything ever written — competent and dead. That is a different teaching moment from a planted error, and it needs its own slot.

3. Where the grid is thin

  • None. Every cell has at least one legal job.

1 further cells have two legal jobs or fewer (shaded cream). Those are the cells where a faculty member has almost no choice, and they are the first place to look when adding a job.

4. The regression cases cluster

The twelve cases sit in 12 distinct cells — 17% of the grid. Real faculty answers concentrate, which is good news: populating the well-trodden cells well matters far more than filling all 72 evenly.
