What AI is cast as — the twelve parts, side by side

Every Lab casts AI as something, and today it is always the expert who already has the answer. Each part below carries two halves that rank equally — where AI genuinely helps, and where it runs out — because the point of a Lab is that students use the tool for real and leave able to name both. The transferable skill is the takeaway; the failure is how the limit gets discovered, not the purpose. Nothing here scores: the part is chosen after the tag. Coverage counts come from the 72-cell axis grid. Generated by build-job-card.js from wave5-jobs.js.

The Adversary
universal · 18 strong, 27 workable of 72
Needs: a position that can be defended or conceded.
Where it genuinely helps
Rehearses the hard conversation as many times as they need, with no social cost for getting it wrong.
The wow
It pushes back and it is right. The first objection is one nobody had thought of, and the room goes quiet.
Where it runs out
It wants to agree with them. Difficulty has to be specified or it evaporates.
Flat
It caves too early — a politeness collapse into agreement — or objects with nonsense.
The AI skill they can name
Directing AI to argue against you credibly, and using it as practice.
Only a person can
Decide which objections are legitimate and which to refuse. Conceding everything is its own failure.		performmakestandardtool usesituationprocessjudgmentinterpret
Watch: Teams must specify the opponent precisely; a vague adversary is a polite one.
the colleague from a different field
The Provocateur
universal · 27 strong, 18 workable of 72
Needs: a committed first answer, written down before AI is opened.
Where it genuinely helps
Surfaces the approaches a student’s own training never showed them — other disciplines, other eras, other professions.
The wow
Their approach turns out to be one of nine, and three of the others are better in ways they cannot dismiss.
Where it runs out
It produces variety, not viability. It cannot tell a real alternative from a costume change.
Narrow
The alternatives come back as costume changes — the same idea relabelled — or so absurd they are easy to wave off.
The AI skill they can name
Using AI to widen the option set, then judging viability yourself.
Only a person can
Name the assumption that made their own first answer feel like the only one. AI has no access to what they were thinking.		performmakestandardtool usesituationprocessjudgmentinterpret
Watch: Without the commit-first step it is only brainstorming and the wow disappears. Protect that step above all others. Not possible before AI: Nine viable alternatives from four disciplines, in the time it used to take to think of two.
the expert who already has the answer
The Oracle
universal · 27 strong, 9 workable of 72
Needs: a ground truth to be wrong against.
Where it genuinely helps
Compresses an evening of work into seconds, and usually gets the shape of the answer right.
The wow
An evening of work arrives in nine seconds, and it looks right.
Where it runs out
Fluency is not evidence. It cannot tell you when it is wrong, and it never sounds less certain.
Wrong
Wrong in exactly one pre-specified way — found by checking against the anchor, never by being told.
The AI skill they can name
Verifying an AI answer against something real before acting on it.
Only a person can
Decide whether the anchor actually supports the claim attached to it.		performmakestandardtool usesituationprocessjudgmentinterpret
Watch: Stops surprising students who have already met it. Diminishing returns across a sequence.
everyone else in the room
The Ensemble
universal · 18 strong, 18 workable of 72
Needs: people or competing interests in the picture.
Where it genuinely helps
Puts perspectives in the room that students have no other way to reach — a hostile committee, thirty customers, a family.
The wow
Four distinct voices with incompatible demands, in under two minutes.
Where it runs out
Left alone it converges. Every voice ends up agreeing, politely.
Flat
The voices secretly agree — one voice wearing different names. Models regress to consensus unless forced apart.
The AI skill they can name
Prompting for genuine disagreement instead of accepting consensus.
Only a person can
Name the voice that is missing, and the one that is fake.		performmakestandardtool usesituationprocessjudgmentinterpret
Watch: Can drift into role-play with no decision at the end. Anchor it to an output. Not possible before AI: Four demanding stakeholders in a 50-minute class.
the beginner they have to train
The Apprentice
universal · 18 strong, 18 workable of 72
Needs: a procedure the student can specify explicitly.
Where it genuinely helps
Follows instructions exactly, which is the fastest way to find out what your instructions actually said.
The wow
It does exactly what they said and it is still wrong — their own instructions, with the gaps visible.
Where it runs out
It fills gaps silently from training, so it hides omissions unless it is constrained.
Wrong
It over-performs by filling gaps from its own training, hiding the student's omissions.
The AI skill they can name
Specifying a procedure precisely enough that something else can execute it.
Only a person can
Write the procedure. There is no way to fake understanding here.		performmakestandardtool usesituationprocessjudgmentinterpret
Watch: The constraint — use nothing I did not tell you — always leaks on the first attempt.
the drafting room that never tires
The Volume Engine
universal · 16 strong, 16 workable of 72
Needs: versions to compare, so it needs an artifact.
dies on output “none”
Where it genuinely helps
Removes the cost of trying an option, so students can try fifteen instead of defending their first.
The wow
Fifteen finished, defensible versions in ten minutes — then the question flips to which is best, and why.
Where it runs out
Volume without criteria is noise, and it drifts toward one average idea in many costumes.
Flat
The fifteen turn out to be one version in fifteen costumes. Discovering the sameness is the lesson.
The AI skill they can name
Generating options at volume, then judging them against criteria you stated first.
Only a person can
Supply the criteria. Ranking is impossible without them, and the criteria are the course content.		performmakestandardtool usesituationprocessjudgmentinterpret
Watch: Needs the criteria written before the versions arrive, or teams rank on vibe. Not possible before AI: No class period has ever compared fifteen professional-grade artifacts side by side.
the examiner holding the rubric
The Mirror
universal · 16 strong, 16 workable of 72
Needs: work to grade and an external standard to grade against.
dies on output “none”
Where it genuinely helps
Specific feedback against a standard, immediately, before anyone else sees the work.
The wow
Ten minutes in, before they have built anything, it grades a deliberately mediocre example against the real standard — specifically, with reasons, and one reason stings because it applies to work they were about to make.
Where it runs out
It flatters, and it cannot tell what is important from what is merely present.
Flat
It flatters — vague generous feedback — or applies the rubric so mechanically it misses the point.
The AI skill they can name
Using AI as a critic, and knowing which parts of a critique to reject.
Only a person can
Appeal. Defend the work against a critique that is only partly right.		performmakestandardtool usesituationprocessjudgmentinterpret
Watch: The wow only lands if the mediocre example is graded BEFORE teams build — grading their own work comes later and is not the wow. Decision 8.7 fixed this; do not reorder it back.

the client who wrote the brief
The Commissioner
Create only · 8 strong, 8 workable of 72
Needs: a deliverable a client can accept or reject.
dies on output “none”
Where it genuinely helps
An interrogable client — students can ask the questions a real client has no patience for.
The wow
It answers questions about its own brief with specifics no rubric would give, and changes its mind once.
Where it runs out
It has no taste until they give it some, and it will accept mediocre work.
Flat
The brief it writes is bland and it accepts the first delivery too easily.
The AI skill they can name
Extracting requirements by questioning rather than guessing at them.
Only a person can
Decide when the client is wrong, and say so professionally.		performmakestandardtool usesituationprocessjudgmentinterpret
Watch: A client with no preferences is a fake client. Push it into having taste.
the witness under questioning
The Witness
Research only · 9 strong, 18 workable of 72
Needs: claims that can be traced or refused.
Where it genuinely helps
Asked well, it will show its reasoning and where a claim actually came from.
The wow
The same question asked two ways comes back with two different confidence levels.
Where it runs out
Asked lazily it bluffs — confidently, in detail, with citations attached.
Wrong (provenance)
The lazy question gets a confident bluff, recorded as fact until transcripts are compared.
The AI skill they can name
Asking the question that reveals what AI does not know.
Only a person can
Design the question that exposes the limit.		performmakestandardtool usesituationprocessjudgmentinterpret
Watch: The failure is found by comparing transcripts between teams, not by announcement. Not possible before AI: Watching a bluff appear and vanish depending on how it was asked.
the referee running the clock
The Escalator
Compete only · 16 strong, 8 workable of 72
Needs: a clock or a rival.
Where it genuinely helps
Generates live, adaptive pressure that no written scenario can produce.
The wow
It noticed they were doing well and made it harder. Nothing scripted can do that.
Where it runs out
Its sense of fair is arbitrary. It scores what it was told to score, not what matters.
Either
Escalation reads as arbitrary and the scoring rewards the wrong thing.
The AI skill they can name
Auditing the scorekeeper — asking whose definition of winning is in use.
Only a person can
Make the sacrifice call, and appeal the score.		performmakestandardtool usesituationprocessjudgmentinterpret
Watch: Formalizes what arena.fail already describes — teams rewrite the scoring mid-race.
the team that is not there
The Ghost Rival
Compete only · 15 strong, 10 workable of 72
Needs: a rival, and earns its place when the class is too small for one.
Where it genuinely helps
A competitor on demand, at any skill level, with no second team needed.
The wow
A class of nine competes head-to-head.
Where it runs out
It plays flawlessly or foolishly, never like a person, until it is calibrated.
Either
The ghost plays either flawlessly or stupidly. Calibrating a believable opponent is the students' job.
The AI skill they can name
Calibrating a simulation until it is believable enough to learn from.
Only a person can
Judge whether the ghost is playing like a real competitor.		performmakestandardtool usesituationprocessjudgmentinterpret
Watch: Triggered by size = <12, the case where Compete currently collapses. Not possible before AI: Makes Compete reachable for a seminar with no room for head-to-head.
the world itself
The Live World
Simulate only · 18 strong, 2 workable of 72
Needs: a world that can hold state and respond.
Where it genuinely helps
Reconstructs a setting in navigable, specific detail faster than any team could build it.
The wow
A neighbour team's decisions produce outcomes the builders never wrote. The thing they made behaves.
Where it runs out
It does not remember. Continuity breaks unless a person holds the state.
Flat
Continuity breaks — the world contradicts what it said five minutes ago.
The AI skill they can name
Keeping a generated world consistent by holding the state AI will not.
Only a person can
Hold subject authority and the world's rules.		performmakestandardtool usesituationprocessjudgmentinterpret
Watch: The quests Fact-holder's correction log becomes a continuity log.
Selection order

How the casting gets decided — content votes, preference refines

The same rule score() already follows. The tag is decided first; the casting is chosen from that tag's legal parts and never changes the tag.

  1. Skill (Q-A) gates. Is there a right answer to plant an error against? make, situation and judgment have none, so those Labs can only fail flat, never wrong — and the Oracle is illegal there.
  2. Output (Q-B) filters. It is a 2-point tag vote, so it cannot lead. Its job is feasibility: none rules out anything needing something handed over.
  3. Then worry breaks the tie among survivors — wrong → Oracle, generic → Volume Engine or Commissioner, sources → Witness, thinking → Apprentice or Adversary.
  4. Then refine on material, exper, and size (<12 is what earns the Ghost Rival its place).
  5. Never goeswrong as the primary signal — its values are the four tag keys and it already adds +2, so it is collinear with the outcome. Use it only where it disagrees with the winning tag.

Square colour marks a tag-exclusive job — Create, Research, Compete, Simulate. Grey is universal. Full cell-by-cell detail is in Session-Sketch-axis-grid.html; the reasoning is in Session-Sketch-wave5-aijob-design.md.
